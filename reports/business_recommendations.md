# Business Recommendations and Action Plan



**Business Case:** NovaCart Global

**Based On:** Analysis of sales, profit, customers, discounts, returns and geography from 2012–2015



## Purpose



The analysis found strong business growth, but it also identified revenue that was generated at very low or negative profit.



The recommendations below focus on practical actions that can protect profit without stopping healthy sales growth.



## Priority Summary



| Priority | Business Issue | Why It Matters | Recommended Action |

|---|---|---|---|

| High | Deep discounts | Discounts above 20% were loss-making overall | Introduce discount limits and approval rules |

| High | Loss-making products | 677 products produced a combined loss of about $247.84K | Review pricing, discounts and fulfilment costs |

| High | Tables sub-category | Tables produced a loss of about $64.08K | Review the full Tables product range |

| Medium | Loss-making countries | 31 countries were loss-making | Review country-level pricing and cost structure |

| Medium | At Risk customers | This group generated about $2.02M in historical revenue | Run targeted customer reactivation campaigns |

| Medium | Seasonal margin pressure | December revenue was strong but margin was lower | Plan promotions using profit targets |

| Ongoing | Returns | Overall returned-order rate was 4.19% | Monitor return patterns by market and product |





## 1. Control Deep Discounts



### Why This Is Important



Discount was the strongest profitability risk in the analysis.



The discount band above 50% generated approximately **$371.62K in revenue**, but produced a loss of approximately **$412.58K** and a **-111.02% margin**.



### Recommended Actions



1\. Allow discounts up to 20% only when the expected profit remains positive.

2\. Require manager approval for discounts between 20% and 30%.

3\. Require pricing or finance approval for discounts above 30%.

4\. Block discounts above 50% unless there is a clear reason, such as approved stock clearance.

5\. Show estimated profit and margin to the salesperson before a discount is confirmed.

6\. Review discount performance every month by product, category, country and salesperson.



### Suggested Responsibility



\- Sales Management

\- Pricing Team

\- Finance Team



### Suggested Timeframe



Start within the first **30 days**.



### Measures to Track



\- Revenue and profit by discount band

\- Profit margin by discount band

\- Number of loss-making sales

\- Value of discounts requiring approval

\- Exceptions approved above the discount limit



### Expected Business Result



This action should reduce avoidable losses while allowing the business to continue using discounts where they still produce positive profit.





## 2. Review Loss-Making Products



### Why This Is Important



The analysis found **677 loss-making products**. These products generated **19.28% of total revenue**, but produced a combined loss of approximately **$247.84K**.



Tables was the main problem sub-category:



\- Revenue: **$757.04K**

\- Profit: **-$64.08K**

\- Profit Margin: **-8.46%**

\- Average Discount: **29.07%**



### Recommended Actions



1\. Create a monthly list of products with negative profit or margin.

2\. Start the review with the Bottom 5 products shown in the Power BI dashboard.

3\. Check each product’s selling price, discount, shipping cost and fulfilment cost.

4\. Reduce discounts on products that already have low margins.

5\. Negotiate supplier or shipping costs where possible.

6\. Test a higher selling price for products with strong demand but poor profit.

7\. Classify reviewed products into three groups:

&#x20;  - **Keep:** Product is already profitable.

&#x20;  - **Fix:** Product can become profitable after pricing or cost changes.

&#x20;  - **Exit:** Product has no realistic path to positive profit.

8\. Do not remove a high-revenue product immediately. First check whether its cost or pricing problem can be corrected.



### Suggested Responsibility



\- Category Management

\- Pricing Team

\- Procurement

\- Supply Chain Team



### Suggested Timeframe



Complete the first review within **30–60 days**.



### Measures to Track



\- Number of loss-making products

\- Total loss from negative-profit products

\- Tables profit and margin

\- Average discount by product

\- Shipping cost as a share of revenue

\- Profit improvement after pricing or cost changes



### Expected Business Result



The business should retain products that support profitable growth and reduce revenue that creates unnecessary losses.



## 3. Improve Geographic Profitability



### Why This Is Important



Asia Pacific generated the highest revenue, while Europe generated the highest profit and strongest market margin.



The analysis also found **31 loss-making countries**. Turkey and Nigeria were among the countries with the largest losses.



This shows that a market can look profitable overall while some Regions or Countries inside it are performing poorly.



### Recommended Actions



1\. Review performance using the following drill-down:

&#x20;  - Market

&#x20;  - Region

&#x20;  - Country

2\. Start with the countries showing the largest losses.

3\. Compare discount levels, product mix, shipping cost and profit margin for each loss-making country.

4\. Check whether the same products are profitable in other countries.

5\. Adjust local prices or discount rules where costs are higher.

6\. Give each Regional Manager a clear list of the loss-making locations under their responsibility.

7\. Review regional progress every month using revenue, profit and margin together.



### Suggested Responsibility



\- Regional Managers

\- Market Managers

\- Pricing Team

\- Supply Chain Team



### Suggested Timeframe



Complete the first country and regional review within **30–60 days**.



### Measures to Track



\- Revenue, profit and margin by Market

\- Revenue, profit and margin by Region

\- Number of loss-making countries

\- Average discount by Country

\- Shipping cost by Country

\- Profit improvement by Regional Manager



### Expected Business Result



This review should help managers find local problems that are hidden inside profitable market totals and make decisions based on the conditions in each Region.



> Regional Manager information is available in the Power BI data model through DimGeography. A separate manager dashboard is not required; the field can support regional reviews, filters and future drill-through analysis.



## 4. Protect Repeat Customers and Reactivate At Risk Customers



### Why This Is Important



Repeat purchasers generated approximately **96.86% of total revenue**.



RFM analysis also identified an At Risk customer group with approximately **$2.02M in historical revenue**. These customers were valuable in the past but showed lower recent purchasing activity.



### Recommended Actions



1\. Create a list of At Risk customers ranked by historical revenue and profit.

2\. Review their last purchase date, preferred category and previous order value.

3\. Contact the highest-value customers first.

4\. Use relevant product recommendations instead of offering the same discount to every customer.

5\. Avoid deep discounts in reactivation campaigns unless the expected profit remains positive.

6\. Use different messages for Consumer, Corporate and Home Office customers.

7\. Track whether reactivated customers make another profitable purchase.

8\. Review customer activity every month using the RFM groups.



### Suggested Responsibility



\- CRM Team

\- Marketing Team

\- Customer Success or Account Management

\- Sales Team



### Suggested Timeframe



Start the first reactivation campaign within **30 days** and review results over **60–90 days**.



### Measures to Track



\- Number of At Risk customers contacted

\- Reactivated customers

\- Revenue and profit from reactivated customers

\- Repeat-purchase rate

\- Average order value by customer segment

\- Customer profit after campaign discounts



### Expected Business Result



The business should protect revenue from valuable existing customers while avoiding unprofitable blanket discounts.



> The 99.87% figure in this project is an observed repeat-purchase rate, not a formal customer-retention rate. Retention cannot be measured accurately without complete customer acquisition and churn data.



## 5. Plan Peak-Season Sales Around Profit



### Why This Is Important



November and December generated the strongest monthly revenue.



However, December’s profit margin was approximately **10.73%**, compared with the higher October margin of approximately **13.41%**. This shows that peak-season revenue did not produce the best margin.



### Recommended Actions



1\. Plan inventory and staffing before the November–December sales peak.

2\. Set both revenue and profit targets for seasonal campaigns.

3\. Review the expected margin before approving seasonal discounts.

4\. Promote profitable Technology and Office Supplies products during the peak period.

5\. Avoid using deep discounts on already low-margin Furniture products.

6\. Compare actual seasonal results with the previous year.

7\. Review revenue, profit, margin and average order value together after each campaign.



### Suggested Responsibility



\- Sales Planning

\- Marketing Team

\- Inventory and Supply Chain Team

\- Finance Team



### Suggested Timeframe



Complete seasonal planning at least **60 days before** the expected peak period.



### Measures to Track



\- Monthly revenue and profit

\- Monthly profit margin

\- Average order value

\- Seasonal discount level

\- Profit by promoted product

\- Year-over-year seasonal growth



### Expected Business Result



Seasonal campaigns should increase sales without creating unnecessary margin pressure.



## 6. Improve Return Monitoring



### Why This Is Important



The overall returned-order rate was approximately **4.19%**. USCA recorded the highest market rate at approximately **4.47%**, although the differences between markets were small.



The current Returns data identifies returned orders but does not identify the exact returned item or the reason for the return.



### Recommended Actions



1\. Continue monitoring returned-order rate by Market, Region and Country.

2\. Review products that frequently appear in returned orders.

3\. Start collecting the exact returned Product ID for each return.

4\. Add a standard return-reason field, such as:

&#x20;  - Damaged item

&#x20;  - Incorrect product

&#x20;  - Late delivery

&#x20;  - Quality problem

&#x20;  - Customer changed mind

5\. Track refund cost, replacement cost and shipping cost linked with returns.

6\. Review USCA first because it recorded the highest market return rate.

7\. Do not assume that every product in a returned order was returned.



### Suggested Responsibility



\- Customer Service

\- Operations Team

\- Product Quality Team

\- Supply Chain Team



### Suggested Timeframe



Begin improved return-data collection within **60 days** and review results monthly.



### Measures to Track



\- Returned-order rate

\- Return rate by Market and Region

\- Return reason

\- Return cost

\- Products appearing in returned orders

\- Delivery time for returned orders



### Expected Business Result



Better return data will help the business separate product-quality problems from delivery or customer-service problems and take more accurate action.



## 7. Test Cross-Selling Opportunities



### Why This Is Important



Exact product pairs did not appear often enough for reliable product-level recommendations.



At category level, two combinations showed useful early signals:



\- Appliances and Paper recorded a lift of approximately **1.17**.

\- Accessories and Phones appeared together in **377 orders** and showed a positive relationship.



### Recommended Actions



1\. Run a small cross-selling test for Accessories with Phones.

2\. Test Appliances with Paper only where the combination is relevant.

3\. Show product suggestions during checkout or in follow-up communication.

4\. Do not use deep discounts to force the bundle.

5\. Compare the test group with normal orders.

6\. Continue the campaign only if it improves order value and profit.



### Measures to Track



\- Orders containing both categories

\- Average order value

\- Bundle revenue and profit

\- Profit margin after promotion

\- Customer response rate



### Expected Business Result



A controlled pilot can show whether the category relationships can increase profitable order value. The current analysis should be treated as a starting point, not proof that every bundle will succeed.



## 8. Suggested 90-Day Action Plan



| Timeframe | Main Actions |

|---|---|

| Days 1–30 | Introduce discount approval rules, create the loss-making product list and prepare the At Risk customer list |

| Days 31–60 | Review Tables, test pricing and cost changes, analyse loss-making countries and begin improved return-data collection |

| Days 61–90 | Review results, expand successful customer campaigns, test selected cross-selling ideas and assign regional actions to managers |



## Final Measures of Success



The action plan should be reviewed using:



\- Overall profit and profit margin

\- Profit margin by discount band

\- Loss from negative-profit products

\- Tables profit and margin

\- Number of loss-making countries

\- Profit from reactivated customers

\- Average order value

\- Returned-order rate

\- Regional profit improvement



## Conclusion



NovaCart Global does not need to stop growing or stop using discounts completely. The main goal is to make sure that future sales also create profit.



The first priority should be discount control. The next priorities should be loss-making products, Tables, unprofitable countries and high-value At Risk customers. Results should be reviewed regularly through the existing Excel and Power BI dashboards.
